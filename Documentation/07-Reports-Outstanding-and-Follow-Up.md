# 07. Reports, Outstanding Invoices, and Follow-Up Routines

This chapter is written for music teachers, private studio operators, and music school administration teams using LessonFlow to run lesson scheduling, student communication, and billing with confidence.

Reporting is most valuable when paired with routine action. In LessonFlow, dashboard metrics and invoice aging filters are not passive visibility features; they are operational triggers. A healthy admin routine uses these tools to decide what requires intervention today versus what can wait.

A practical daily cycle starts with outstanding invoice review by aging bucket. Early-stage overdue invoices usually respond to lightweight reminders, while older debt often requires stronger owner-visible follow-up. The goal is to prevent silent accumulation by acting while balances are still recent.

Weekly review should compare period trends rather than isolated totals. If cancellations rise while booking volume is stable, scheduling or customer communication may need adjustment. If outstanding totals rise while invoice volume remains normal, reminder cadence or payment friction may be the root cause.

Reports should be interpreted in operational context. Metrics tell you where to look, not automatically what to do. Pair report findings with booking calendar observations and customer note quality to identify practical corrective actions.

Use reminder actions in controlled batches and document unusual responses. A predictable sequence produces better outcomes than irregular bursts of follow-up activity. Over time, consistent cadence improves both collection speed and customer expectations.

Effective financial follow-up in LessonFlow is a rhythm: inspect, segment, act, verify, and repeat.
