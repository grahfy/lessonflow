# 03. Booking Management

This chapter is written for music teachers, private studio operators, and music school administration teams using LessonFlow to run lesson scheduling, student communication, and billing with confidence.

<div class="manual-callout success">
<strong>Operational Core:</strong> The bookings console is the primary decision surface in LessonFlow and should be reviewed before billing and reporting each day.
</div>

Booking operations begin with understanding status intent. Pending requests represent unconfirmed demand and require explicit approval or rejection logic. Approved bookings are active instructional commitments and may trigger additional tasks such as communication updates, material assignment, or invoice preparation. Cancelled records remain valuable historical evidence and should not be treated as disposable noise.

Calendar navigation controls allow different planning horizons, but the operational habit should be consistent: inspect near-term workload first, then scan medium-term congestion, then process outstanding request decisions. Day and week views are best for immediate execution. Month and year views help identify emerging capacity or clustering patterns that may require proactive schedule balancing.

When approving a request, validate identity and context before action. Check name and contact details, confirm lesson mode and duration, and review notes for constraints that could affect teaching logistics. Approval should only occur when the result is executable by the school. If details are incomplete, edit or communicate before confirming.

Editing a confirmed booking should preserve clarity for everyone who depends on the record. If time changes, use the dedicated move flow and verify new time persistence after save. If notes change, write concise context that another operator can understand without external conversation history. If the booking links to customer or billing actions, confirm those links remain intact after edits.

Cancellation handling must be precise. Operators should communicate policy consistently and avoid ambiguous status changes. In LessonFlow, cancellation remains an auditable transition, which protects historical accountability. If a cancellation occurs near lesson time and fee rules apply, document the context clearly and ensure downstream billing logic reflects policy.

The booking detail panel provides workflow shortcuts including customer open, invoice/billing entry, and learning materials management. Use these shortcuts deliberately to keep process continuity. Jumping out to unrelated pages without completing the booking context often causes missed follow-up actions.

At the end of each booking action, confirm three outcomes: the record saved correctly, the visible status is expected, and any required communication or billing follow-up is explicitly handled. That closeout habit is the difference between reactive admin work and reliable operations.
